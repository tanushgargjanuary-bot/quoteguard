If you price by feel, you already know the problem

Most cleaning businesses don’t fail because they can’t clean. They fail because they quoted a big house at a small-house price, absorbed a supply run they never billed for, and found out three weeks later that a full month of work cleared almost nothing.

QuoteGuard exists because “it felt like a fair price” is not a pricing method. It replaces that instinct with arithmetic you can defend to a customer and audit against your own bank account.

What it actually does

Five connected parts. You set it up once; every quote after that takes minutes.

1. Setup

Your business defaults in one place: labor rate per cleaner-hour, overhead, target margin, minimum job price, processing fee percentage.

2. Quote calculator

Enter square footage, crew size, hours, condition, and add-ons. It returns a price that hits your target margin — or your floor, whichever is higher.

3. Job records

Every quote is frozen as static values. Changing your labor rate next month does not silently rewrite what you quoted last month.

4. Job review

After the job, enter what you actually spent and earned. QuoteGuard shows the variance between quoted expectation and realized profit.

5. Client quote

A clean, printable page you hand or email to the customer. No raw numbers, no formulas, no apologies.

6. Scenario testing

Model what happens before you commit: heavier soil, an extra cleaner, a discount, a bigger add-on. See the margin move before you say yes.

The math is the product

QuoteGuard is not a calculator with a price field. The price is solved — derived from your costs and the margin you need to hit, then checked against a floor you set. You never type a price by hand, which is the whole point.

Every real cost gets counted

Labor scaled by crew size, hours, and a soil-condition multiplier. Supplies. Travel. Overhead. Add-on materials. Card processing fees treated as a genuine expense, not an afterthought.

The margin is enforced

The quote is built backward from the margin you need, so the number that comes out is one that actually pays. If the inputs can’t support your floor, you find out before you commit — not after.

Nothing is left to memory

Each quote is stored as a fixed record, so your pricing history stays honest even after you change your rates. Adjusting next quarter’s labor rate cannot quietly rewrite what you charged last quarter.

You find out what it really made

Enter actual costs and revenue after the job, and QuoteGuard shows the gap between the margin you quoted and the margin you kept. That gap is where cleaning businesses quietly bleed.

That last part is what most operators never do at all. Quoting profitably gets you half way; knowing which jobs failed to deliver it is what stops you repeating the mistake.

Built-in guardrails

  • Margin target enforced, not suggested. The price is solved from your costs and target margin, so you cannot accidentally quote below it.
  • A hard price floor. No quote can come out below your minimum job price, no matter how the inputs look.
  • Processing fees counted as a cost. Card fees are treated as a real expense, which most home-brew quote sheets ignore.
  • History that cannot drift. Quotes are stored as frozen values, so your records stay true as your defaults change.
  • Input validation. Condition multipliers and margins are bounded, so a typo fails loudly instead of producing a confident wrong price.

Who this is not for

Being straight with you saves us both time. QuoteGuard is probably not a fit if you run a single client on a fixed weekly retainer, if you price strictly by the hour with no materials involved, or if you have never once wondered what your actual margin on a job was. It is built for operators who quote variable jobs — varying square footage, crew, and condition — and who need those quotes to be profitable on purpose rather than by luck.
